Copper, Cohorts, Crisis

Zambia's Demographic Trajectory across 3 Regimes (1964-2024)

Executive Summary

This infographic explores why Zambia's demographic transition stalled despite massive copper wealth and educational expansion. Using data from the Wittgenstein Centre, UN World Population Prospects, and World Bank, I analyze three political-economic regimes to show how privatized extraction prevented the domestic investment needed for demographic transformation. Botswana, which retained state control of diamond revenues, serves as a counterfactual.

Key Findings

  • Population Growth: 3.7M (1965) → 19.1M (2020) = 416% increase
  • Education Paradox: Secondary/post-secondary attainment visible by 2020, but fertility stayed at 4.1 children/woman
  • Life Expectancy Crisis: Fell 7 years during structural adjustment (1990-2000), recovered via external donors (PEPFAR, Global Fund)—not copper revenues
  • Health Financing: Government spending stayed flat despite copper boom; external aid peaked at $137/capita (2019)
  • Inequality: Gini rose from 49.1 (1998) → 51.5 (2022) as elites captured mineral rents
  • Botswana Counterfactual: State control of diamond revenues enabled fertility decline to 2.7 (vs. Zambia's 4.1)

Methodology

All visualizations created in R using:

  • wcde package for population pyramids by education
  • wpp2024 for UN demographic indicators
  • wbstats / WDI for World Bank economic data
  • ggplot2 + patchwork for data visualization

It applies world systems theory and demographic transition theory to understand why resource-rich Zambia experienced different outcomes than neighboring Botswana.
